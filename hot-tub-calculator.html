Why hot tubs need more chlorine than you'd think

A hot tub holds a few hundred gallons of 100 °F water shared by several people, so chlorine is used up quickly: one soak can burn through 2–3 ppm. Testing before and adding chlorine after every use keeps the water clear and stops odors.

Dichlor is the usual choice for spas because it is pH-neutral and easy to measure, but it also adds stabilizer: about 0.9 ppm of CYA for every 1 ppm of chlorine. Once CYA climbs past 40–50 ppm, switch to liquid chlorine for a while or drain and refill, which most owners do every 3–4 months anyway.
